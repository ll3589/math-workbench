Steps

    Order of operations PEMDAS

    1. Parentheses
    2. Exponents
    3. Multiply and Divide, left to right
    4. Add and Subtract, left to right
    3 + 4 × 2 = 3 + 8 = 11 (not 14) (3 + 4) × 2 = 7 × 2 = 14

    Place value

    thousandshundredstensones.tenthshundredthsthousandths
    4321.567

    Places after the point end in -ths. 0.5 = five tenths, 0.07 = seven hundredths.

    Comparing decimals: line up the points and add zeros. 0.5 vs 0.45 → 0.50 vs 0.45, so 0.5 is bigger.

    Rounding

    Look at the digit just to the right of the place you're rounding to.

    • 5 or more: round up
    • 4 or less: keep it the same
    3.47 to the nearest tenth → 3.5 $12.632 to the nearest cent → $12.63

    Decimal rules

    + −
    Line up the decimal points. Fill gaps with 0.
    ×
    Multiply as whole numbers. Count the decimal places in both numbers; the answer gets that many.
    ÷ whole
    Point goes straight up into the answer.
    ÷ decimal
    Move both points right until the divisor is a whole number.
    × 10, 100
    Move the point right (1 place per zero).
    ÷ 10, 100
    Move the point left (1 place per zero).

    Fraction rules

    Simplify
    Divide top and bottom by the same number.
    + −
    Same bottom first. Then add or subtract the tops. The bottom stays.
    ×
    Top × top, bottom × bottom.
    ÷
    Keep, change, flip: keep the first, change ÷ to ×, flip the second.
    Mixed → top-heavy
    Bottom × whole + top, over the same bottom.
    Whole number
    Write it over 1: 5 = 5/1.

    Change mixed numbers to top-heavy fractions before you multiply or divide.

    Divisibility shortcuts

    Handy for simplifying fractions.

    2

    3

    4

    5

    9

    Percent problems

    part = percent × whole 20% of 45 → 0.20 × 45 = 9
    Tax, tip
    Find the percent, then add it to the price.
    Discount
    Find the percent, then subtract it from the price.
    % change
    (new − old) ÷ old, then × 100.
    10% trick
    Move the point 1 left. 10% of $64 = $6.40, so 20% = $12.80.

    Ratios & proportions

    A ratio compares two amounts: 3 to 4, 3:4, or 3/4. A proportion sets two ratios equal. Solve by cross multiplying.

    3 cans cost $4. How much for 12 cans? 3/4 = 12/x → 3 × x = 4 × 12 3x = 48 → x = 16 → $16

    Unit rate = amount for 1. $4 for 3 cans is about $1.33 per can.

    Negative numbers

    + −
    Think of a number line or money owed. −5 + 8 = 3. Subtracting a negative is adding: 6 − (−2) = 8.
    × ÷
    Same signs → positive. Different signs → negative.
    −4 × −3 = 12 −12 ÷ 3 = −4

    Exponents & square roots

    An exponent says how many times to multiply a number by itself. 5² = 5 × 5 = 25. 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8.

    A square root undoes a square: √49 = 7 because 7 × 7 = 49.

    Solving equations

    Get x alone. Undo each step with its opposite, and always do the same thing to both sides.

    3x + 5 = 20 subtract 5: 3x = 15 divide by 3: x = 5 check: 3 × 5 + 5 = 20 ✓

    3x means 3 × x. Opposites: + and −, × and ÷.

    Measurement

    Length
    12 in = 1 ft · 3 ft = 1 yd · 5,280 ft = 1 mile
    Weight
    16 oz = 1 lb · 2,000 lb = 1 ton
    Liquid
    8 fl oz = 1 cup · 2 cups = 1 pint · 2 pints = 1 quart · 4 quarts = 1 gallon
    Time
    60 sec = 1 min · 60 min = 1 hr · 52 weeks ≈ 1 year
    Metric
    kilo = 1,000 · centi = 1/100 · milli = 1/1,000. 1 m = 100 cm = 1,000 mm. 1 kg = 1,000 g.

    Big unit → small unit: multiply. Small → big: divide.

    Geometry formulas

    Perimeter
    add all the sides (distance around)
    Rectangle
    A = length × width
    Square
    A = side × side
    Triangle
    A = ½ × base × height
    Circle
    C = π × d (or 2πr) · A = π × r² · π ≈ 3.14
    Box
    V = length × width × height
    Cylinder
    V = π × r² × height

    Radius is half the diameter. Area is in square units (ft²), volume in cubic units (ft³).

    360°

    Test-day habits

    • Estimate first, so you can spot an answer that's way off.
    • Check: does the answer make sense in the story? A person can't buy 2.4 cars.
    • Work backward from the answer choices when you're stuck.
    • Check the units the question asks for: inches or feet, dollars or cents.
    • Skip a hard one and come back to it. Don't leave any blank.

    Some tests, and some sections of a test, give you a formula sheet. Check what your test allows. Knowing these by heart still saves time.

    Blank boxes

    Find where a row and a column meet, and multiply. Click a box to light up its row and column. Press Enter to move to the next blank box.

    Every problem is new each time, so there is always more practice. Press Enter to check an answer, and Enter again for the next one. Progress counts are kept in this browser only.
